# Prepositions

## Min | من 

Core Functions: origin, source, starting point, comparison, cause.

English equivalent: From/out of/than.

Examples:

  - Ana Bashte8el min el-bait | I work from home.
  - Ba7eb el-shai aktar min el-2ahweh | I like tea more than coffee 
  - Ana ta3ban min el-shu8ul | I’m tired from work.

## 3ala | على

Core functions: Location, destination, and time/event.

English equivalent: On/at/to.

Examples:

  - El-akel 3ala el-tawla | The food is on the table.
  - Ana baru7 3ala el-bait | I go home. 
  - Ana bashrab 3asir 3ala el-3asha | I drink juice at dinner. 

## Fi | في

Core functions: being inside something physically or conceptually (place, time, condition), as well as “there is”

English equivalent: Inside/in/there is.

Examples: 

  - Ana bashte8el fi America | I work in America 
  - Ana banam fi-ellail  | I sleep at night. 
  - Fi mushkeleh fi hai el-8assaleh | There is a problem in this washing machine

## Ma3 | مع

Core functions: accompaniment / shared presence (togetherness)

English equivalent: With.

Examples:

  - Ana baru7 3ala el-shu8ul *ma3* sadiqi | أنا بروح على الشغل مع صاحبي - I go to work *with* my friend.
  - Huwwe byakol El-akel *ma3* 3aseer | هو بياكل الأكل مع عصير - He eats the food *with* juice.
  - Humme byeeju *ma3i* | هم بييجوا معي - لThey come *with* me.

## Bi | بِ

Core Functions: Location/inside / means / instrument/tool / ngagement

You are operating within something or through it.

English equivalent: By/with/in

Examples: 

  - Ana baaji *bi*-lseyyara | أنا باجي بالسيارة - I come by car.
  - Huwwe ma bye7ki *bi-*ltelefon | هو ما بيحكي بالتلفون - He’s not speaking on the phone.
  - Humme byaklu *bi*-el-shoakeh u el-sekkineh | هم بياكلوا بالشوكة والسكينة - They eat with a fork and a knife.

## La | لَ

Core functions: Possession / recipient / purpose / end point 

English equivalent: For/to.

Examples:

  - Hada el-ktab *la*-mehdi | هاد الكتاب لمهدي - This book is for Mehdi.
  - Huwwe beynam min el-lail *la*-lsubu7 | هو بينام من الليل للصبح - He sleeps from night to morning.
  - Ana bat3allam 3arabi *l*a2dar a7ki ma3kom | أنا بتعلم عربي لأقدر أحكي معكم - I learn Arabic to be able to speak with you.
  - Enti laazem te7ki*l*o enno huwwe naaje7 | أنتِ لازم تحكيلو انو هو ناجح - You should tell him that he’s successful.
  - Hadi el moazeh *eli* mish *elo* | هادي الموزة الي مش الو - This banana is mine not his.  

# Noun Possessives

In spoken Arabic, possession is usually shown by putting two nouns next to each other.

No apostrophes. No “of”. Just noun + owner.

We switch the words: first the the main noun, and then what/who we’re owning it to.

## Proper Nouns Pattern (Names, places, etc.):

put the thing + the name. No *el*- on the first noun. Proper nouns make it definite automatically.

  - Mehdi’s book = Ktaab Mehdi.
  - California Street = Share3 California.
  - John’s Shirt = Bloozet John.

If the first noun is feminine, we add “t” to the end of it before linking it to the possessive noun.

## Nouns with Pronouns Pattern

put the thing + the pronoun-ended noun. No *el*- on the first noun nor the second. Pronouns make it definite automatically.

  - My hand’s finger = Usba3 eedi.
  - My friend’s room = 8urfet sadiqi. 
  - My family’s house = Bait 3ailti.

## Common/Regular Nouns Pattern

First noun loses el-; second noun decides definiteness. 

  - Work clothes = Lebes el-shu8ul
  - Home food = Akel el-bait
  - Kitchen’s table = Tawlet el-matba5.

## Chain Possession Pattern

You can link multiple owners in a row. Keep the same structure: thing → owner → owner → …

The first noun never takes el-; only the last noun decides definiteness.

  - My friend’s sister book = Ktab u5t sadiqi.
  - The car’s work chair = Kursi seyyaret el-shu8ul
  - The family’’s Bedroom’s light = Dawu 8urfet noam el-3aile.

## Describing Professions 

When describing a profession, we first put the the profession, then what specification we’d like to add to it.

  - Doktoar snaan = Dentist / Teeth doctor.
  - M3allem 3arabi = Arabic Teacher
  - Ustaaz Farsi = Farsi teacher. 

When we want to refer to these people with the, we add “el” to the specialty/specification.

  - Doktoar el-3yoon = The eyes Doctor
  - M3allem el3arabi = The Arabic teacher
  - Ustaaz el-englizi = The English professor 

If we add a pronoun to the first noun, The pronoun already makes the word specific. So no el is needed.

  - Doctoarhom elsnaan = Their dentist 
  - M3almi el3arabi = My Arabic teacher.
  - Ustaazo elfarsi = his Farsi Professor 

# Noun + Adjective definition 

In Arabic, Adjectives go after the noun.

  - Big house = Bait kbeer
  - Old car = Seyyara 2adeema 
  - Clean room = 8urfe ndeefa

When the noun is specific or known, add el- to the noun. If we’re describing the noun, we don’t add “el” to the adjective.

*If the noun is ends with a pronoun, it’s deffinitive by default. No “el” is needed.*

  - The girl is beautiful = El-bet 7elwa
  - This shirt is long sleeve = Hadi el-blooze kum
  - Our family is big = 3ailetna kbeera

If we want to define the noun, we add “el” to the noun and adjective.

  - The new clothes = el-lebes el-jdeed.
  - The high chair = el-kursi el-3aali
  - The expensive dress = el-fus6aan el8aali.

# Time and Clock

In Arabic , Time can mean different things: 

  - The actual time: Waqt
  - The clock: Saa3a
  - turn : Marra 

So we say saa3a to refer to the clock. *Saa3a* can also mean **hour** and **watch**. 

To say the time is …, we start with “**El-saa3a**” and then say the number in it’s feminin form. So nothing changes besides *one* and *two*, because after 2 numbers are feminin (talate, arba3a, etc.). 

  - One (F): Wa7de
  - Two (F): Tentain.

So **the time is one** = **El-saa3a wa7de,** and **the time is two** - **el-saa3a tentain. The time is three = El saa3a talaate, and so on.**

**To describe minutes, we just add “u/w”** and say the number of minutes. 

  - El-saa3a talaate u 3ashara = The time is 3:10 
  - Elsaa3a wa7de u 5amse = The time is 1:05
  - Elsaa3a tentain u 3eshreen = The time is 3:20.

Complementary Words: 

  - Rube3 = Quarter / Quarter after.
  - Noss = Half / Half passed 
  - Ella rube3 = Quarter to. 

In the case of 1 and 2 minutes, we have to use the words for a minute, and 2 minutes.

Unlike English, where words can only be singular and plural,  in Arabic we have singular, dual, and plural forms of words.

  - d2ee2a: a minute.
  - D2ee2tain: 2 minutes.
  - d2aaye2 : minutes (3+) 
  - Thaanie / saanie = A second.
  - Saaneitain = 2 seconds.
  - Sawaani = Seconds (3+). 
  - Saa3a = an hour.
  - Saa3tain = 2 hours.
  - Sa3aat = Hours (3+).

So **it’s 3:02** = **El-saa3a talaate u d2ee2tain,** and **it’s 1:01** = **Elsaa3a wa7de u d2ee2a**. 

If we want to say *at* a certain time, like **at 2:00**, we use  the preposition “***3ala.***” so **At 10:00** = **3ala elsaa3a 3ashara**.

If we want to asj about the time, like **What time is it?, we use either:**

  - Qaddaish / 2qaddaish el-saa3a 
  - Shu el saa3a 

To ask what / which **time is …**, we use “***ay.”*** *so* ***What time is dinner?*** *=* ***Ay saa3a el-3asha?***

# Adjectives

In Arabic adjectives usually take “min” as preposition.

  - I’m scared of you = Ana 5aayef minnak/ek
  - She’s is upset with him: Heyye mitdaay2a minno. 
  - They’re embarrassed of us: Humme ma7roojeen minna.

Other adjectives take other prepositions:

  - Worried (2al2aan/e/a) = 3ala.
  - Missing/longing (Mishtaa2) = la
  - Different (Mu5talef/e/a / 8eir) = 3an.

## Plural forms

Excited 

To make an adjective (specifically emotions) plural we do the following:

  - Masculine/general plural: We add “een” as a suffix 
  - Feminine plural: We add “aat” as a suffix.

Humme mabsoo6een = They’re happy. (M)

Humme 7anoonaat = They’re compassionate (F). 

## Use of “Bakoon”

In spoken Palestinian Arabic, “bakoon” usually means:

  - I am usually / I tend to be
  - I will be
  - I would be (depending on context)

It adds habit, future, or conditional meaning.

### Normal present

In Arabic, you don’t need “to be” in the present tense. (No bakoon)

  - I’m tired = Ana ta3baan
  - He;s ready = Huwwe jaahez

### Bakoon for Habitual meaning 

Bakoon + adjective = repeated state.

  - Ana bakoon kasool ba3ed el-shu8ul = I tend to be/usually tired after work
  - Marrat heyye betkoon ju3aane billail = sometimes she’s hungry at night
  - E7na benkoon mash8uleen yoam el-etnain = We’re busy on monday

### Bakoon for Future Meaning

  - Bukra beykoon mabsoo6 = Tomorrow he’ll b happy.
  - Ra7 akoon mitwatter el-usboo3 el-jaay = I will be nervous next week.

### Bakoon in Conditional Sentences

  - Anabakoon mitdaay2a iza ma btiji ma3i = I am/’d be upset if you don’t come with me.
  - Heyye betkoon mad8oo6a lamma ykoon 3endha shu8ul kteer = She’s stressed when she’ has a lot of work.

**“when” and “if” are an indicator to conditional sentences.**

# Use of Kul

In Arabic, “kul” can mean both:

  - Every / each.
  - All.

To make it easier, here is the rule to how to use it depending on what you mean:

  - All = kul + “el”
  - Every / each = Kul + no “el”

E.G.

  - Every day = Kul yoam 
  - All day = kul el-yoam 
  - Everyone = kul 7ada
  - All people = kul el-naas
  - Every week = Kul usboo3 
  - All week = Kul el-usboo3 

# Verb “B” Prefix Elimination 

In Palestinian Arabic, the b- prefix (e.g., ba7ki, bitshoof, biroou7) is the marker of the Present Indicative. It’s for things that are real, habitual, or currently happening.

## When do we drop the b?

We drop the b- (when the action is uncertain, desired, or dependent on another word.

### For Intent and Necessity

We drop the b if we see the following before it:

  - Biddi (I want): Biddi ashrab  = I want to drink. 
  - Laazim (Must/should): Lazim tertaa7 = You should rest. 
  - Mumkin (Can/May): Mumkin asa3dak? Can I help you?.

### Time Conjunctions

If we’re connecting the following time conjunctions before the verb, the b is no longer needed.

  - Abel-ma (Before): Abel-ma yiju  = Before they come.
  - Ba3ed-ma (After): Ba3ed-ma teshra7i = After you explain.
  - Lamma (When): Lamma a6la3   = When I go out. 
  - Ra7 (Will): Humme ra7 yjibu el-3asha ma3hom = They will bring dinner with them. 

### Purpose (To / In order to)

Words that explain why or how you are doing something will also strip the "b-" from the following verb.

  - La- (purpose to) Beddi aru7 3ala el-dukkan la-ashtri khobez. = I want to go to the shop to buy bread.
  - 3ashaan (in order to) Ana beddi ashufo 3ashan a7kilo eshi  = I want to see him to tell him something.

### Compound Verbs

When two verbs are stacked together, the first one carries the tense/mood, and the second one doesn’t take a “b”

  - Ana baballesh amshi min el-saa3a sab3a el-sube7 = I start walking from 7:00 in the morning
  - Huwwe beyjarreb ye3mal ryaada aktar = He tries to do sports/work out more
  - E7na ma bne2dar n5alli el-baab maftoo7 bellail = We can’t let/keep the door open at night.

### Chain Reaction

When you start a sentence with a "Gatekeeper" word (like Biddi, Ba7eb, ra7, Laazim), you are entering a "Zone of Desire/Intent." Everything connected by the word "u" (and), “wala” (nor), or  “aw” (or) inside that zone stays with no b-).

Even if you put a long description between them, the rule holds:

  - Ana ba7eb a66alla3 3ala el-njoom u a7ki ma3ha = I like to look at the stars and talk to her.
  - Huwwe ma beddo yesma3 7ada aw yshufna 3asaan huwwe mitdaaye2 = He doesn’t want to listen to anyone or see us because he’s upset.
  - Biddi arou7 3al-dokkan el-kbeereh el-ba3eedeh u ashtri khobez. = I want to go to the big far-away shop and buy bread.
  - E7na laazem nemshi aktar bil-6abee3a u nshemm hawa ndeef = We should walk in nature more and smell clean/fresh air.
  - Ma ba3ref a7ki 3arabi wala aktubo = I don’t know how to speak Arabic nor how to write it
  - Beddak tiji 3alinna aw e7na niji 3alaik = Do you want to come to us or we come to you.

## When do I keep the “b?”

These are the instances where you must keep the b-, even if there is a connecting word.

### The “Enno” Exception

The word Enno (That) acts as a wall. It starts a brand-new "real" sentence. If the action is a fact or a feeling about reality, the b- stays.

  - Ana bazonn enno ma bey7ebna = I believe/think  that he doesn’t like us.
  - Ba3raf enno bteshte8li hoan = I know that you (F) work here.

# Pointer Rule

In Arabic, if you put the object (the thing or person) at the very start of your thought, the verb feels "empty." You have to fill that empty spot with a small pronoun (-o for him/it or -ha for her/it) that points back to the start.

In simpler terms: 

In English, we often leave a "gap" after a verb or preposition. We say, "The book I read" or "The man I talk to."

In Arabic, you cannot leave that gap. If you mention a person or a thing before the action, you must leave a "pointer" (a shadow pronoun) to link the action back to them.

When would the object precede the verb in the sentence ?

## Superlative

When you use words like Aktar (Most), A7san (Best), Aswa2 (Worst), or A2al (Least), you are putting that noun in the spotlight. You must "point" back to it.

  - Aktar eshi basawwih = The thing I do the most
  - A7san wa7de ba3rafha = The best one (F) I know. 
  - Shu aswa2 tamrin bte3malo = what is the worst exercise you do?
  - Hadi a2al akleh bnetbo5ha = This is the meal we cook the least. 
  - Min a7san zboon bteshte8el ma3o = who is the best customer you/she work with?

## Any/Which & Every

Words like Ay and Kull always move the object to the front of the sentence.

  - Ay 7ada bashufo = Anybody I see.
  - Kul kursi byishteruh = Every chair they buy. 
  - Ay makaan benru7 3alaih = Every place we go to. 
  - Kul sadiq bnelte2i ma3o/fiyyo = Every friend we meet. 
  - Ay mat3am betfadlu taaklu fih = Which restaurant do you guys like to eat in?

## Introducing (indeffinite) Nouns

Whenever you introduce a new item, most likely to be indeffinitive (without “el”), or a person using, the verb that follows must have a pointer.

  - Fi zalame ma ba3rafo barra = There’s a man I don’t know outside.
  - 3endo tilfizion ma byista5demo = He has a TV he doesn’t use.
  - Lamma tlaa2u eshi bet7ebbuh = When you guys find something you like.
  - Beddhom felem ye7daruh = They want a movie to watch
  - Laazem tlaa2u eshi te3maluh = You guys should find something to do.
