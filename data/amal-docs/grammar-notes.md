### A2+3. Possessive Endings 
If a noun ends with a pronoun in the case of possession for example, it’s considered definitive thereby does not take “el” - bait u5ti = My sister’s house, bent u5t jaarti = my neighbors niece. 
A4. possessive endings
  - Biddi (to want) and 3indi (to have) conjugate by using these possessive forms because they’re not real verbs. E.g. Biddo = he wants, 3indna = we have. 
  - If a word ends in a vowel: 
\-ak turns into -k, Ek turns into -ki, And -o turn into prolonged vowel and “h” at the end. 
E.G. Kursik = your (m) chair, a3tuki = awa3iki = your (F) clothes, abuh = His father. 
  - Possessive pronouns are also used to add pronoun objects to verbs. The only difference is that instead of -i for me, it’s -ni. Give me = a3tini, and a3tih = give him. Etc. 
  - There are few words that end with “ain” mostly in dule form that have a certain way to make possessed. The words are:
 (2) hands = Eed = eedayy, eedaik, eedaiki, eedaikom, eedaih, eedaiha, eedaina, eedaihom
(legs) ijer = ijrayy, ijraik, ijraiki, ijraikom, ijraih, ijraiha, ijraihom, ijraina. 
 (ears) daan = denain = dinayy, dinaik, dinaiki, dinaikom, dinaih, dinaiha, dinahom, dinaina.

Not every dule form is treated like this. So you can’t say youmayy for my 2 days for instance. These are the words that are treated as such and will be updated if we learn another word that follow this pattern. 
### B2 -4“b” drop 
  - The actual rule is if a verb is followed by another, the second one loses the “b” 
  - Another model  words, such as desire or intent biddi, laazem, mumken, jaay 3abali, time words like lamma,  ra7, ba3ed/2abel ma, or purpose like la- and 3ashaan. 
  - Another case is if the verb is perceived by a statement like: it’s nice, it’s important, most likely, etc. so muhem te3raf = it’s important to know, 3ala el-a8lab niji = we most likely/probably will come. ETC. 
  - Iza (if) keeps the b. It doesn’t require us to lose it.

### B7. kan + verb
  - Along with meaning used to, it can also mean past continuous, like I was reading = Kunet aqra2, she was cooking = kaanat tetbu5.
  - The note about “b” drop is not necessarily correct in all cases. Although sometimes it’s ok to be kept, I advise against marking this as a rule. We can say in this case ispecifically the “b” drop rule is not very strict. Some people keep it, others drop it. 
  - Add Kaan laazem. Kaan laazem means should have and had to. Depending on the tense of the verb following it the meaning is determined. If it’s past, it means should have, but Kaan laazem + present can mean both should have and had to. I advised using it with the present tense so it’s easier and less confusing.
### B8. bikoon 
  - Add habitual use of bikoon. When paired with usually, sometimes, every…, ETC. 3aadatan bikon mash8ool 3ala el-wa7de = HE’s usually busy at 1:00. 
  - Add bikoon use with to want and to have in past and future forms, and when paired with lamma or ize (conditional). E.g. kaan biddi = I wanted, ra7 ykoon 3indi = He will have, lamma ykoon biddak = when you want, iza bikoon 3indak = if you have. The conditional case is not very strict and you may hear people not including bikoon. In past and future it is required. 
  - Bikoon can also mean probably. Bikoon m3asseb halla = he’s probably angry now. Bitcoin teshte8el = she’s probably working.

### C6 if and when 
  - These are conditional words. They require a “bikoon” before the adjective or biddi and 3indi in the sentence they’re in. 
  - While “lamma” drops the present indicator “b,”  “iza” does not. 
### C7 Illi 
  - Add the no el no illi rule. Meaning if the word prior to illi is not definitive by “el” or a pronoun possessive, we don’t add “illi” to it. E.G. aktar ishi ba7ebbo = The thing I like the most, seyyarti illi basoo2ha = my car that I drive, fi u8niyye ba3rafha = There is a song that I know. 
### C8 Question tools 
  - Addaish and kam both mean how much/many. They’re just used differently and have different sentence structure. 
  - Kam always takes singular form: E.G. kam dars = how many lessons. 
  - Kam also means few. 3indi kam ishi = I have few things
