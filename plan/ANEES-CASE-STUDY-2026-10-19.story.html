The goal

An app that lists the mistakes the tutor corrected

  • After each recorded lesson, list every mistake the tutor corrected, so the student studies a list instead of replaying the hour.
  • Three puzzles stood in the way: hearing, counting and grading.
  • 46 days (Sept 3 – Oct 18, 2026), 24 recorded lessons; AI coding helpers did the engineering.

Puzzle 1 · Hearing

Speech-to-text "autocorrects" the learner

  • Speech-to-text writes what he should have said, so his mistake disappears.
  • Method A: ElevenLabs writes first, then Gemini 3.8 Flash re-listens to each of his lines 3 times; 2 of 3 agreeing wins.
  • Every other way scored lower. A separate test of Gemini 4 Argon as the listener also scored lower (84% and 79%).

Method A: ask three times, keep the majority

  • Like asking three friends what they heard and going with the majority.

Puzzle 1 · Hearing

The danger: copying the teacher

  • The re-listener hears the tutor say the right word a second later and writes it for the student. His mistake vanishes.
  • The guard: any change that makes him sound like the tutor waits for the tutor to check.
In the app: Tutor › lines she checksIn the paper: 5.3 Hearing
Puzzle 2 · Counting

A mistake = he says it wrong AND the tutor corrects it

  • Doesn't count: asking how to say a word, using English, fixing it himself first, a correct local form, anything she let pass.
  • Only what the tutor actually corrected is counted.

Puzzle 3 · Grading

Even the answer key had mistakes

  • Answer key = the right answers, like a teacher's answer sheet. The student re-listened to 3 lessons line by line.
  • He re-checked 4 of his own marks: 2 were wrong. 6 mistakes were counted twice under two labels.
  • The 20 extra came from one AI (Gemini 4 Argon). The tutor is now checking them blind.

Finding mistakes · Results

Each side wins at something

  • Today's reader (in the app now): two AIs, Claude Opus and Sonnet; a mistake is kept only if both flag it.
  • Judge panel (tested only): many AIs flag, 1–2 judge AIs vote on each flag.
  • Precision = of what it flagged, how much was real. Recall = of the real mistakes, how many it found. Scored on the student's first key (99).

Finding mistakes · Results

One score, but an unfair test

  • F0.5 = one 0–100 score that mixes both, with precision counting extra (a false "you made a mistake" breaks trust).
  • On the final key (119), Opus + Argon led. But 20 of those 119 were Argon's own finds: like grading a student with a key they helped write. Not proven.

Lessons learned

Money: a slip-up and a new rule

  • A paid test drained credits with no spending limit.
  • New rule: monthly plans first; paid only with a dollar cap set ahead of time.

What comes next

Pooled blind review by the tutor

  • Pooled = every AI's finds go in one pile. Blind = the tutor can't see which AI found it, like a taste test with labels hidden.
  • A winner only counts if it wins again on 3 new lessons. Search-engine test sets (TREC) are built the same way.
